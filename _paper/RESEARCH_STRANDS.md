# Narrative strands and research questions

*20 narrative strands · 55 research questions*

**Goal:** explain air quality's relevance to human health to non-specialists.
Candidate opening: “What do air measurements tell us about the places we live
in, and why do they matter for health?”

**F = narrative strand; RQ = specific research question.** Each RQ appears in
full once; related-question links connect strands. **All strands: to discuss**
unless explicitly overridden. Pollutants, period, sample, coverage and methods
remain to be chosen against the main question.

## Decisions

- [ ] Agree a main health-related question and connected strands.
- [ ] Check pollutant availability; justify period, indicators and coverage criteria.
- [ ] Verify health literature; distinguish ambient measurements from personal exposure and health outcomes requiring additional information.
- [ ] Run initial descriptive analysis; revise the path as evidence develops.

## Strand details

### F01

**Station types based on pollution levels**

**Guiding question:** Which places have similar concentration profiles?

- <a id="rq1"></a> **RQ1.** Can homogeneous groups of Italian monitoring stations be identified from PM₂.₅, PM₁₀, NO₂ and O₃ concentrations?
- <a id="rq3"></a> **RQ3.** Can stations in geographically distant regions have similar multipollutant profiles?
- <a id="rq5"></a> **RQ5.** Are clusters based on individual pollutants consistent with those obtained by considering several pollutants together?

### F02

**Profiles, official categories and geography**

**Guiding question:** How well do official categories and geography describe the observed differences?

- <a id="rq2"></a> **RQ2.** Do clusters identified from pollutant concentrations correspond to official station classifications (Traffic, Industrial, Background), or do different groups emerge?
- <a id="rq26"></a> **RQ26.** How different are pollution profiles at Urban Traffic, Urban Background and Suburban Background stations?
- <a id="rq27"></a> **RQ27.** Under comparable weather conditions, do Traffic stations have systematically higher NO₂ concentrations than nearby Background stations?
- <a id="rq28"></a> **RQ28.** Are stations in the Po Valley more sensitive to atmospheric stagnation than stations in other Italian macroareas?
- <a id="rq29"></a> **RQ29.** Do coastal, inland and mountain stations have different ventilation regimes and particulate matter profiles?
- <a id="rq30"></a> **RQ30.** Are vegetation characteristics, represented by LAI indices, associated with differences in PM₁₀ and PM₂.₅ concentrations after controlling for weather and geographical context?
- <a id="rq31"></a> **RQ31.** Are differences between clusters explained mainly by geographical location or by stations' environmental and meteorological characteristics?

### F03

**What the average hides**

**Guiding question:** Do stations with similar averages differ in peaks and variability?

- <a id="rq4"></a> **RQ4.** Does including indicators of variability and extreme concentrations (P95, P99, IQR) identify different clusters from those obtained using mean concentrations alone?

### F04

**Weather, accumulation and differences in response**

**Guiding question:** Which conditions accompany high concentrations, and how much do these associations vary across stations?

- <a id="rq7"></a> **RQ7.** How strong is the association between boundary layer height (BLH) and PM₂.₅, PM₁₀ and NO₂ concentrations across Italian stations?
- <a id="rq8"></a> **RQ8.** Are there groups of stations particularly sensitive to atmospheric stagnation, characterised by weak winds and low BLH?
- <a id="rq9"></a> **RQ9.** Does the relationship between wind speed and PM₁₀ concentrations vary with geographical context, suggesting regimes dominated by dispersion or by dust resuspension and transport?
- <a id="rq10"></a> **RQ10.** Is precipitation associated with reductions in particulate matter of different magnitudes at urban, suburban and rural stations?
- <a id="rq11"></a> **RQ11.** Does relative humidity modify the relationship between ventilation conditions and PM₂.₅ concentrations?
- <a id="rq12"></a> **RQ12.** Do stations with similar mean PM₂.₅ concentrations respond significantly differently to changes in weather?
- <a id="rq13"></a> **RQ13.** Can station clusters be identified from their weather sensitivities rather than their absolute concentrations?

**Related questions:** [RQ28](#rq28) (F02).

### F05

**The pollution calendar**

**Guiding question:** Which seasonal patterns distinguish places, even when annual averages are similar?

- <a id="rq14"></a> **RQ14.** Can clusters of stations with different seasonal profiles of PM₂.₅, PM₁₀, NO₂ and O₃ be identified?
- <a id="rq15"></a> **RQ15.** Can stations with similar annual mean concentrations show significantly different seasonality?
- <a id="rq16"></a> **RQ16.** Is the strength of PM₂.₅ seasonality associated with the frequency of atmospheric stagnation?
- <a id="rq17"></a> **RQ17.** Do seasonal pollutant profiles vary systematically with elevation, latitude and station type?
- <a id="rq20"></a> **RQ20.** Is seasonal-cycle amplitude more discriminating than annual mean concentration for some station categories?

## Analysis planning checks

When selecting a strand, add the relevant checks below to its plan (or link to
this section and state the selection). Apply PM, SD, directional-weather and
municipality checks only when those variables/products are used. Record sample,
criteria, outcomes and evidence under the selected strand; mark checks complete
only for that analysis, not globally. No strand is selected yet.

- [ ] **Keys/dates/grid:** daily + SD counts, duplicates, malformed/range dates,
  partition overlaps; expected station/date grid; absent rows vs missing values.
- [ ] **Joins:** unmatched IDs, key cardinality, unchanged row counts.
- [ ] **Coverage:** pollutant × station × year/season valid days, long gaps,
  first/last valid dates, never-measured targets; distinguish inactivity, gaps,
  cleaning and imputation where possible. NA ≠ zero; check NA/NaN/empty/non-finite parsing.
- [ ] **Sample/indicators:** question-based inclusion/common-period criteria and
  sensitivity; paired valid days for pollutants, PM ratios, Traffic–Background;
  match units/averaging periods to references; document episode thresholds and missing-day rules.
- [ ] **Values:** negatives, impossible weather, extremes/time-series anomalies;
  min≤q1≤median≤q3≤max and min≤mean≤max with rounding tolerance. Do not automatically delete plausible extremes.
- [ ] **PM:** identical summaries by site/year; paired fine/coarse comparability,
  measurement methods where available, PM₂.₅>PM₁₀ and small/zero denominators;
  no silent ratio clipping or removal of negative differences.
- [ ] **SD:** unique keys, nonnegative finite values, unmatched keys, target alignment;
  justified sensitivity analysis, not inference that absent/zero SD means error-free data.
- [ ] **Weather:** validate compass mapping/timing before directional/lag claims;
  shared meteorology, collinearity, temporal/spatial dependence; check other variable ranges.
- [ ] **Time/geography:** network turnover vs within-station change, static metadata
  limitations, unusual periods (e.g. 2020); RDS schemas/keys/dates/code coverage before municipality analysis.
- [ ] **Reporting:** save code, derived tables, exclusions, sample/period and
  denominators; attach evidence/outcome before marking checks complete.

Definitions, verified facts and dataset limitations remain in `DATA.md`.
